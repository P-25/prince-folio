Every wire on the board is an arrow, and every arrow wants out. Tap one and it flies off in the direction it points — but only if nothing is in its way. Get it wrong and it slams into whatever is blocking it, and you lose a life. The whole game is that one rule.

The boards are packed. Long winding wires fold back on themselves, thread past each other and lock together like a circuit board. Somewhere in there is a wire with a clear run to the edge. Find it, clear it, and the board opens up a little more.

What it plays like

Unhurried. There is no timer counting down and no move limit. Take as long as you like. Levels start small and get properly big, from four straight arrows on the first board to a 60×60 grid with over two hundred wires on the last. Pinch to zoom when a board fills the screen.

Every level is drawn by hand, not generated. Some are packed squares; others are shapes — a heart, a sword, a skull, a submarine, a tank — which changes how the whole board reads before you touch it. And there is a daily challenge for when you have finished the rest.

Fair by design

Nockways is free, and it stays out of your way. There are no banner ads and nothing pops up between levels. The only ads in the game are ones you tap on purpose — an extra hint, or a second chance when you run out of lives — and if you never tap them, you will never see an ad.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. There is no account to make, no server and no analytics: your progress is saved on your own phone, and it goes away when you uninstall.
